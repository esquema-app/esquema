# The lighthouse of Isla Ejemplo

## Chapter 1: The arrival

Marta arrived at Isla Ejemplo on a foggy Tuesday, having just turned thirty, with a suitcase full of books. The lighthouse had been dark for two winters and nobody in the village wanted to climb its one hundred and twelve steps. Tomás, the oldest fisherman on the pier, handed her the key without a word.

That night Marta found, under the stairs, an unopened letter addressed to the last keeper. She put it in her coat pocket and decided she would read it when the lighthouse shone again.

## Chapter 2: The light

For three weeks Marta cleaned the glass, changed the lamp oil and learned to read the sky. Tomás came by every afternoon with fish and news from the village: the school was closing, the ferry came only on Thursdays and the young people were leaving for the mainland.

On the first Friday of March the light turned again. From the pier, people clapped as if a ship had arrived. Marta felt that the island had finally accepted her.

## Chapter 3: The storm

In April the worst storm in forty years arrived. Marta, who at forty-five had seen many, had never felt the lighthouse shake like that. She spent the whole night by the lamp, feeding it by hand when the wind cut the power.

At dawn, a boat appeared on the rocks. It was Tomás's. Marta ran down the one hundred and twelve steps and found him alive, holding on to a net, looking at the light that had guided him.

## Chapter 4: The village

After the storm, the village decided the lighthouse would never go dark again. They opened a reading room on the ground floor with Marta's books, and the school children came to learn to read the sky.

Tomás, with no boat left, stayed on as her helper. Every night, before lighting the lamp, Marta told him a story. The island, which used to look towards the mainland, started looking towards the light.
